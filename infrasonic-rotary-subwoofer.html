ENGINEERING / CASE STUDY

Infrasonic Rotary Subwoofer

Iterative mechanical design and electrical troubleshooting on a custom low-frequency sound system.

ORGANIZATION
Engineers Without Borders · Lamar University
ROLE / STATUS
Team Member
August 2026–Present · Ongoing
FOCUS
Prototyping · 3D printing · Diagnostics

01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Project overview

As a member of Lamar University’s Engineers Without Borders organization, I have been working with a team to design and build an infrasonic rotary subwoofer from the ground up.

With no prior team experience designing this type of system, we have researched existing designs, manufactured custom components, and worked through unexpected mechanical and electrical problems. My contributions include implementing a driveshaft stabilization solution, proposing a second support structure, and using electrical measurements to help diagnose a failure.

02 / CHALLENGE 01: INITIAL DRIVESHAFT INSTABILITY

Challenge 01: Initial driveshaft instability

During early testing, we identified driveshaft instability as the system approached its target operating RPM. Our team researched existing designs, investigated potential causes, and evaluated possible solutions.

We developed a custom fitting and bearing system to stabilize the shaft. I helped develop the solution and personally installed the manufactured components.

Result: The system operated stably at the target RPM, allowing us to proceed to the next testing phase.

03 / CHALLENGE 02: OSCILLATION INSTABILITY

Challenge 02: Oscillation instability

During a later fully loaded test with the new bearing housing, the assembly became unbalanced when the blades began to oscillate.

I proposed two support struts running from opposite sides of the stabilized outer rim, through notches in the housing, to a central bearing around the driveshaft. Together, the struts formed an X-shaped support. The bearing was intended to provide stability while allowing the shaft to move axially during blade oscillation.

We 3D printed the components, installed them, and tested the updated assembly.

Result: The added support system resolved the instability observed in that test.

04 / CHALLENGE 03: ELECTRICAL FAILURE

Challenge 03: Electrical failure

After attaching the assembly to a baffle, the subwoofer stopped operating. We spent hours trying to identify the problem.

Drawing on my electrical apprenticeship experience, I used a multimeter to investigate voltage and resistance. As the operating frequency was adjusted, I observed readings ranging from an open-circuit indication to approximately zero ohms and around one ohm. These unusual readings helped us suspect a problem involving the speaker coil, although the measurements alone did not establish the cause.

We disassembled the subwoofer and found a loose screw magnetically attached inside the unit. It had rubbed through protective mesh and contacted the coil, creating a short circuit.

Result: We addressed the issue and restored the subwoofer to operation. The full rotary mechanism has not yet been reassembled and retested, so that final verification remains ahead.

05 / WHAT I LEARNED

What I learned

This project has shown me how closely mechanical design and electrical reliability are connected. Solving one problem often reveals another, and each iteration has required us to test assumptions against the behavior of the real system.

I especially value being able to apply skills from my electrical apprenticeship to a university engineering project. It reinforced the importance of systematic troubleshooting, practical measurement, and knowing how to move between different engineering disciplines.

The project is ongoing, and our next major milestone is retesting the fully reassembled rotary system.
