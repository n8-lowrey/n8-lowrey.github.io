LEADERSHIP / CASE STUDY

From the Bench to Team Captain

Four seasons of growth, competition, and learning how to bring out the best in teammates.

ORGANIZATION
CHSA Wolverines
ROLE / STATUS
Elected JV A and varsity captain · Four seasons
FOCUS
Team leadership · Film review · Youth coaching

01 / STARTING FROM BEHIND

Starting from behind

I joined the CHSA Wolverines as a freshman with no athletic experience and almost no understanding of basketball. I started on the JV B team and rarely played.

I was smaller and less athletic than many of the players around me, but I discovered that I could learn quickly by studying the game. I listened to coaches, observed teammates, and worked to understand plays and situations. My coaches recognized my understanding of the game as a strength.

02 / EARNING RESPONSIBILITY

Earning my place

I spent the summer after freshman year training hard to improve my athletic ability and skills. By sophomore year, I moved to JV A and was elected captain, helping lead practices and advise teammates.

The next season, I joined varsity and was again elected captain. I began organizing film reviews of our own performances and opposing teams, helping lead practices, and preparing teammates to execute our strategy.

03 / LEADING OTHERS

From player to coach

By senior year, coaches trusted me to help coach younger 10U, 12U, and 14U teams. Teaching younger players pushed me to explain techniques and strategy clearly instead of assuming everyone understood the game the way I did.

04 / A LEADERSHIP CHALLENGE

Learning to lead different personalities

One of my hardest challenges as captain was working with teammates who didn't share my level of competitiveness. Winning was what made basketball fun for me, and I struggled when others didn't approach practices or games with the same urgency.

Early on, I sometimes let frustration shape how I responded to mistakes. I learned that getting angry didn't help teammates improve. Specific, constructive criticism—explaining what went wrong and showing how to do better—produced better results.

I didn't need to lower my standards. I needed to learn how to communicate them in ways that helped different people improve.

05 / TEAM ACHIEVEMENTS

Team achievements

Across four seasons, our teams won Division III regional and national championships during my freshman year. During my junior varsity season, we finished as regional runners-up. In my senior year, we won the Division II state championship and Division III national championship and finished as Division II regional runners-up.

06 / REFLECTION

What basketball taught me

Basketball taught me how to push myself beyond what I thought I was capable of. Starting behind my peers fueled my determination to learn and improve.

Leadership taught me that pushing others requires more than determination. It requires patience, understanding, and knowing how to bring out the best in people.

I couldn't control how much experience or natural athletic ability I started with. I could control how hard I worked, how much I learned, and what I did with that knowledge.
